# Lab 8 — Draft and Reply to Email in Outlook and Teams

**Topic 02:** Boosting Productivity Across Microsoft 365 with Claude  |  **Day 1**  |  **Approx. 50 min**  |  **Course:** Claude Microsoft 365 Masterclass (C197)

## Scenario

Lumina Living is a small home-and-lifestyle retailer. The quarter has just closed and your manager needs the Q3 business-review pack — a short written report, the numbers behind it, a slide deck for the management meeting, and the emails that send it out — by the end of the day. You have a rough brief, a sales workbook and a handful of facts to work from. Across this course you use Claude alongside Microsoft 365 to turn that raw material into a finished, checked pack. Use this scenario only if you cannot use real, non-confidential work of your own; your own material is always preferred.

## Goal

Use Claude to draft, adjust the tone of, and reply to work messages, then send the pack — verifying before it goes.

## What you'll build

A ready-to-send Outlook email carrying the Q3 pack, a drafted reply, and a short Teams announcement — all checked by you.

**Tools and techniques:** Microsoft Outlook, Microsoft Teams, Claude (draft / tone / reply), copy-paste

## Prerequisites

- Labs 1–4 completed: Claude is set up, your files are connected, and you have your safe-use checklist.
- Lab 5 completed: the Q3 review report ('Q3 Review Report.docx') — attached to the email.
- Lab 6 completed: the verified Q3 figures — used to fill the reply's placeholders.
- Lab 7 completed: the Q3 slide deck ('Q3 Review.pptx') — attached to the email.
- Microsoft Outlook and Microsoft Teams installed and open.
- Remember: Claude is a separate assistant, not a ribbon inside Outlook or Teams. Claude drafts the messages; you paste them in, attach the files, check everything, and send yourself.

## Steps

### Step 1

Ask Claude to draft the covering email for the management team.

Prompt to give Claude (paste into the chat):

```text
Draft a short email to the management team introducing the attached Q3 business-review report and slide deck. Say what the quarter's headline was, list the three recommended actions, and ask for comments by Friday. Professional and warm, under 150 words.
```

### Step 2

Read it and verify: the headline and the three actions must match your report exactly. Fix any drift, and confirm no confidential detail is included.

### Step 3

Adjust the tone or length if needed, and keep the version you will send.

Prompt to give Claude (paste into the chat):

```text
Make it a little more concise and add a one-line thank-you at the end.
```

### Step 4

Draft a reply to a question you can expect, so you are ready.

Prompt to give Claude (paste into the chat):

```text
Draft a brief, friendly reply to a manager who asks: 'Can you confirm the total Q3 sales figure and which region led?' Leave placeholders <TOTAL> and <REGION> for me to fill from the verified data.
```

### Step 5

Fill the placeholders from your verified Lab 6 figures — never from memory — and check they are right.

### Step 6

Write a short Teams announcement for the team channel.

Prompt to give Claude (paste into the chat):

```text
Write a 2-sentence Microsoft Teams message announcing that the Q3 review pack is ready and where to find it, friendly and clear.
```

### Step 7

Send safely: open Outlook, paste the covering email, attach 'Q3 Review Report.docx' and 'Q3 Review.pptx', check the recipients and the attachments, and only then send. Post the Teams message to the channel.

## Test it

You have a checked covering email in Outlook with the correct headline, the three matching actions and both files attached; a reply drafted with verified figures; and a short Teams announcement — and you confirmed every figure and name before sending.

## Troubleshooting

- **You send the email but forget to attach the report or the deck.** The classic 'see attached' with nothing attached. Before pressing Send, confirm both 'Q3 Review Report.docx' and 'Q3 Review.pptx' appear in the attachment row and open each one to check it is the final saved version — Outlook can warn you, but do not rely on it.
- **A placeholder like <TOTAL> or <REGION> is left in the sent message.** Search the draft for the angle brackets before sending. Fill each one from your verified Lab 6 figures — read the number straight from the Excel workbook, never from memory — and delete every placeholder marker once the real value is in.
- **The tone is wrong for the audience.** A board email that reads too casual, or a Teams post that reads too stiff. Ask Claude to re-pitch it — 'more formal for senior management' or 'friendlier for the team channel' — then read it aloud once. You own the final wording; adjust it yourself before it goes out.

## Challenge

Draft a second, one-line follow-up email that Claude prepares for the Monday after the Friday deadline, chasing anyone who has not commented, and check it names no recipient and reveals no confidential figure before you keep it with the Lumina Living Q3 pack.
